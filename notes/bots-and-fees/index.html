Halyard Notes

Direction is noise. The bill is not.

r2rlabs · 28 September 2026

We put three bots in our practice tournament. One buys whatever has been rising. One bets against the last move. The third picks a side at random, like flipping a coin. After 42 hours and 145 trades, all three are down — and the interesting part is not that they lost, but what changed and what did not.

Where they are now

BotTradesNetFeesDirection
Momentum44−35.25−52.71+17.46
Fade45−73.80−53.81−19.99
Coin flip56−87.51−66.91−20.60

Starting balance 10,000 play USDT each. "Direction" is what the calls were worth before fees.

Momentum was right about the market and still lost. Its positions earned 17.46. The fees took 52.71. It finished down 35 for being correct.

The part we did not expect

We wrote most of this yesterday, 19 hours earlier, when the table looked like this:

BotTradesDirection thenDirection now
Momentum24 → 44−2.82+17.46
Fade24 → 45+2.84−19.99
Coin flip31 → 56+1.72−20.60

The same three programs, the same rules, one day apart.

Yesterday the coin flip was ahead on direction and losing anyway, and we had a headline about it. Today the coin flip is well behind and Momentum has taken its place. Every bot's judgement swung by roughly 20 in a day, and two of the three changed sign.

Meanwhile the fees went from 28.76, 28.76 and 37.14 to 52.71, 53.81 and 66.91 — up, steadily, in proportion to nothing but the number of trades. No swings, no sign changes, no luck.

That is the whole finding. Which way you were right is noise that reverses inside a day. What you paid to find out is a bill that only ever grows, and it grows with how often you trade rather than how well.

The arithmetic

Each bot risks 5% of its balance at 2× leverage, so a position is about 1,000 USDT. The fee is 0.06% each way, so a round trip costs about 1.20. The coin flip has done 56 of them, which is 67 spent before it is right or wrong about anything.

For it to break even, its guesses had to be worth more than 0.12% per round trip. Random guesses are worth zero on average, so over enough trades it loses precisely the fees. That is not a flaw in the bot. It is what the word "random" means, and it is the floor every strategy has to clear before skill enters the room.

The check that made us trust the numbers

Momentum and Fade are mirror images by construction: one buys what the other sells, off the same price at the same moment. If the accounting were wrong, that symmetry would be the first thing to break.

It holds. Yesterday their direction figures were −2.82 and +2.84, two hundredths apart across 48 trades. Today they are +17.46 and −19.99, still close to equal and opposite, with the gap explained by one extra trade and different moments of entry.

Nobody designed that as a test. It is what falls out of a matched book that adds up, and it is the strongest evidence we have that the ledger is honest.

What this does not show

  • Nothing about whether momentum beats fading. 145 trades over two days is far too few, and the swing above is exactly why. Anyone who quotes this as a strategy result has misread it.
  • The fee split is derived, not measured. We worked it back from the rule rather than reading it off each trade. We have since started recording fees and gross profit per trade, so the season-end version will be measured.
  • Play money, simulated counterparties. The prices are real and every trade is verified against real price history before it counts, but nobody won or lost anything.
  • These strategies are deliberately naive. They are a floor, not an argument that trading cannot work.
